EU Wildlife Trade Regulations Reference Guide

A comprehensive reference guide on the rules governing the trade of wildlife into, out of, and within the European Union.

About

Section 1
How do I use this guide?
An introduction to the guide's structure and how to navigate EU wildlife trade regulations effectively.

Reference Guide

Section 2
What species are covered by the Regulations, and in what way?
Understanding which species are covered by the three CITES Appendices and the level of trade protection each provides.
Section 3
What are the rules governing trade into and from the EU for species covered by the Regulations?
Obtaining the correct import permits, export permits, re-export certificates, and import notifications for listed species.
Section 4
What rules govern internal EU trade?
Understanding which species require certificates for trade within the EU and how to obtain and use them.
Section 5
What are the rules governing transport, keeping and movement of live specimens?
Following IATA and CITES guidelines when transporting, keeping, and moving live specimens to ensure their welfare.
Section 6
What are the rules regarding marking of specimens?
Understanding which specimens must be uniquely marked and how marking prevents fraud and controls illegal trade.
Section 7
When can permits and certificates be issued retrospectively?
Circumstances where permits and certificates can be issued after the fact, and how to apply in exceptional cases.
Section 8
Validity, replacement and amendment of permits and certificates
Understanding validity periods, how to replace lost or damaged documents, and when amendments can be made.
Section 9
Can specimens be traded through any Customs office?
Finding designated customs offices for trading specimens and understanding specific requirements for live animals.
Section 10
How are the Regulations enforced?
How Member States monitor compliance, investigate infringements, and apply sanctions under Articles 14, 15, and 16.
Section 11
How are CITES duties organised at national and EU levels between the relevant authorities?
Identifying national management and scientific authorities responsible for implementing CITES and issuing permits.
Section 12
What information must be provided by Member States and the Commission?
Reporting obligations for Member States, including annual trade reports and public information requirements.

This is a revised and updated version, based on the previous edition of the Reference Guide to the European Union Wildlife Trade Regulations originally produced in 1998 by the European Commission, TRAFFIC Europe and WWF.

This document does not necessarily represent the opinion of the European Commission and is not a legal interpretation of European Union legislation.

The contents of this document may be freely reproduced provided that the source is adequately recorded: European Commission and TRAFFIC (2025). Reference Guide to the European Union Wildlife Trade Regulations. Brussels, Belgium.

More details and information relating to the implementation and enforcement of CITES and the EU Wildlife Trade Regulations can be found on the website of the European Commission or by contacting the relevant authorities in EU Member States.
